# Complete account observations under a shared venue budget

The previous per-request weighted gate prevented provider 429s, but a complete account read could spend weight on its first stages and then expire while waiting for its later stages. The backend's 20-second HTTP timeout also raced the bridge's 20-second observation timeout, sometimes replacing a typed venue retry hint with `EXECUTOR_UNAVAILABLE`. The production baseline showed two portfolios alternating between fresh and stale snapshots, including a sampled age of 187.7 seconds. The monitor additionally processed unrelated users serially.

Account observations now register a FIFO interest before sending their first venue request. Insufficient capacity returns a typed retry immediately; a retry retains its place for 120 seconds. An admitted observation holds unused credit until it finishes, protecting its later stages from cheaper background requests. Public sources may use spare credit. New observation shapes reserve a full configured window; successful observations learn the peak actual charges plus bounded request reservations, with a metadata margin and a minimum sufficient for a bounded history request. Only numeric demand is retained. Completed account data is not cached or stitched across attempts. Failed initial observations retain the full-window demand; unexpected growth fails closed before an unaffordable send and increases the next reservation.

Unknown response surcharges still acquire an exclusive hold and settle their complete real weight, including debt. Their temporary full-window hold does not become a learned account footprint. An observation cannot release an already-sent request or refund its charges when it closes. Owner and interest tables are bounded; abandoned interests expire. The budget remains per process and both Hyperliquid hosts share it; this is not a distributed or dedicated-IP quota guarantee.

Admission pacing uses the mean actual charges of the last five complete observations against an eighty-percent capacity target. Forecasting the next slot when a frame starts gives queued owners a precise retry instead of repeated one-second polling. The weighted rolling gate still enforces the hard cap for all reads; this target leaves source headroom without guaranteeing source throughput. A demand estimate can shrink only after three complete observations, retaining the largest of those three peaks plus its margin and minimum reservation. Failed observations do not train a smaller demand. An admitted frame can use two bounded account wire reads concurrently; sources retain one wire slot and unknown responses still require global exclusion. Known siblings wait for a temporary unknown hold to settle before its provisional weight can be mistaken for unaffordable frame growth. A valid persisted short venue retry hint can wake its user before the normal five-second monitor interval.

Account evidence keeps its 20-second complete-observation boundary and each venue request keeps its 12-second wire boundary. Only `account`, `preflight`, and `bind` bridge HTTP calls receive a 25-second transport margin so typed bridge failures can arrive; fetch and JSON decoding share that bound. Write operations retain their existing deadlines. A snapshot timestamp reflects its earliest actual financial evidence, including a reused short-lived mids observation, rather than labeling it with the completion time. Earlier mode guards do not supply financial timestamps; account mode is verified again before completing the observation. Cached mids retain their original wire-dispatch evidence time separately from their unchanged response-based TTL. No evidence TTL is extended.

The monitor uses at most two independent user ticks, with no same-user overlap. Each user's next due time is measured from its own completion, so a slow user does not hold a batch barrier in front of a healthy user. Shutdown stops new ticks and drains existing work. Existing per-user transactions, durable execution identifiers, reconciliation, and notification retries remain in force.

Source collection can overlap an account read. Before using a source for decisions, the runner refreshes a result older than five seconds and verifies source and position timestamps again. If the account expires during that refresh, it rejects the decision. Late collection cannot overwrite a newer source timestamp. No freshness, scope, ownership, concentration, or other risk requirement is relaxed.

Validation and hosted evidence for this change are recorded separately in the task's `outputs/mcp-queue-freshness-20261029/` directory. Synthetic tests do not establish original customer execution acceptance. No App version or package release is part of this change.

FIFO-only validation: 671/671 tests with an isolated PostgreSQL database, zero failures or skips; 53/53 local functional checks; type checking, build, package verification and diff checks passed. The sustained real CoreBridge fixtures cover three owners, complete unified foreign-DEX scopes or standard RWA scopes, history, realistic 500ms venue latency and a rolling 1000-weight cap. Each owner completes repeated observations; source fixed-history reads also progress under the bounded offered load. These heavy synthetic workloads have observation intervals around 100–120 seconds by design, so they establish fair progress under the offered capacity rather than claiming all heavy accounts can always remain within a 30-second execution freshness window. Execution still rejects expired evidence.

The subsequent `6ce846f` hosted observation exposed a capacity limit. One eligible 60.199-second natural window charged 772 account weight while completing two frames, with zero provider 429s. The aggregate mean was 386 per frame; this is report-level attribution, not individual portfolio accounting. Two such accounts refreshed twice per minute need at least 1544 account weight per minute, before sources, against the configured 1000. Fair scheduling alone cannot meet that load's 30-second freshness requirement.

The follow-up read path replaces the foreign-DEX state and order-list REST sweep used by the unified account exposure guard. Orders alone save weight but leave ten serialized state requests, adding five seconds at 500ms per request. A new WebSocket connection for each frame therefore subscribes to both `clearinghouseState` and `openOrders` for the resolved account and every required DEX. An acknowledgment alone proves no account evidence. Every DEX must return both complete payloads with an explicitly matching user and DEX; only then can the batch establish whether foreign exposure or orders exist. No completed snapshot or partial batch is reused. This follows the documented [subscription and data envelopes](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/websocket/subscriptions). A missing state or orders scope, invalid envelope, disconnect or three-second timeout discards the entire batch and falls back to both original weighted REST sweeps. The frame keeps its original deadline and earliest-evidence timestamp, already established by its initial native REST reads. Native financial state, native orders, mode checks and required history retain their existing fresh reads.

For an already-bound unified account without configured RWA scopes, the read stages prioritize its native financial state, mids and spot balance. All these responses must arrive before fixing a common history end and starting the mandatory histories; history is never intentionally cut off before financial evidence. Funding starts first so its unknown response can settle before the bounded fills and flow reads share the frame's two wire slots. The returned fills, funding and flow fields retain their original meanings and common fence. This keeps the bounded fills reservation near the beginning of the charged prefix. Metadata, native orders, the complete foreign-DEX guard and final mode verification remain mandatory. Standard, RWA and bind paths retain their existing history ordering. Every history still covers its ledger start through its explicit fence, with all required pages. Moving the history fence to the frame's beginning would be unsafe: offsetting funding and deposits can leave quantities and equity unchanged while silently changing attributed profit.

For ten foreign DEXes, avoiding ten state and ten order-list REST calls saves 220 weight per complete frame under the [official weights](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/rate-limits-and-user-limits). The projected 166–186 per frame leaves roughly 888 weight per minute for four 186-weight frames and 144 source weight. This estimate does not prove freshness: metadata misses, history surcharges, reservation peaks and fallback can consume the remaining capacity. The separate process-wide WebSocket gate allows twenty connection attempts per rolling minute, two active sockets, eight user identities per minute, one thousand outbound messages per minute and at most sixty-four DEXes per batch, with both subscription messages counted; failures retain attempt credit. These limits leave headroom below the published per-IP connection, subscription, user and message caps, but do not coordinate other processes sharing an IP. A fresh socket does not create a new quota. Hosted evidence after deployment must still establish repeated complete observations within the unchanged execution freshness window; local fixtures and the projection cannot substitute for that acceptance.

The final sustained synthetic fixture runs the real CoreBridge pipelines for 360 seconds with a 120-second warmup: two owners, all ten foreign DEXes, complete required histories, two public sources sampled every five seconds and a fresh 300ms WebSocket batch per frame. With 500ms REST latency, maximum financial snapshot ages are 29.3 and 29.5 seconds; with 300ms REST latency they are 27.9 and 27.3 seconds. Actual provider REST weight peaks at 838 in a rolling minute. These measurements include the age of the previous snapshot until its replacement completes, rather than only timing successful requests. They establish this fixture's bounded workload, not arbitrary latency, scope count or original customer execution acceptance.

Final source validation for the complete WS, staging and pacing change: 728/728 tests with isolated PostgreSQL, zero failures or skips; 53/53 local functional checks; type checking, build and package verification passed. Tests include the actual `ws` client against a loopback server, handshake cancellation, complete dual-channel batches, history-fence cash-flow counterexamples, mode changes and original-dispatch timestamps for cached mids. Original-account execution acceptance remains separate from these checks and from hosted snapshot freshness.

The first hosted WS rollout (`9d3c3c0`) still missed the freshness acceptance by a small margin: after warmup, the largest sampled ages were 30.573 and 33.256 seconds despite zero 429, deadline or transport failures. The second portfolio contained eleven historical failed Perps tasks while all account fill responses in three windows were empty. Account construction nevertheless queried every historical task serially solely to construct an OID map that an empty fill list could never consume. Empty fill histories now skip that unused lookup loop. Nonempty fill attribution, the three history completeness flags and the runner's independent pending/unknown execution reconciliation remain unchanged. Regression tests cover five hundred historical tasks with no fills, a real-shaped nonempty USDC fill with exact attribution, and incomplete non-funding history that must still report incomplete coverage.

A precise single-session operational cleanup removed a retired deployment's state read stuck in PostgreSQL `ClientWrite` before its transaction callback had begun. The session identity, query, wait state, age and ownership were revalidated atomically; only that session was terminated. This restored the second portfolio's natural reads without rewriting saved state. The existing statement and driver timeouts were verified effective in a separate same-constructor connection; they cannot be assumed to control a removed process's orphan connection.

Post-cleanup sampling then exposed an independent scheduler edge: a valid short venue retry could expire while the rest of the user transaction, unknown-execution reconciliation or notifications finished. The runner discarded an already-past deadline, or added another five seconds after the whole tick finished. That produced occasional 31–32-second observation intervals even when venue capacity was available. Validated deadlines now survive callback and commit latency; after all work in the tick drains, a past deadline makes the next tick due immediately. An uncaught tick failure clears the hint and retains ordinary backoff, preventing an expired hint from causing a hot error loop. Invalid hints, same-user exclusion, both transactions, unknown execution identifiers and notification handling are unchanged. Real runner regressions cover each delay boundary and error backoff rather than mocking the scheduling result.

During final package verification, a fresh install selected Redis 6.3.1 but its referenced client/JSON subpackages were temporarily unavailable from the registry. The direct Redis dependency is pinned to 6.0.0, matching the already-installed and locked runtime version; no Redis runtime upgrade is introduced. Package verification checks the resulting clean installation.
